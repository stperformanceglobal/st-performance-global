The Mark

Our story.Your journey.

Every performance journey runs across two kinds of terrain. There is the mountain, climbed patiently and methodically, usually in weather nobody chose. Then there is the road, where the plan runs uninterrupted and you can put your foot flat.

The mark holds both, because every athlete, coach, practitioner and organisation lives in both. You rarely know when the next climb is coming. You rarely know when it will flatten out again. Sometimes you can see the change arriving and sometimes you are already in it before you notice.

What can be controlled is preparation. One terrain always leads to the other, so the work is building people and systems that read the change early and adapt to it. The mountain should not be merely endured and the road should not be merely coasted.

Prepared for either terrain, which is what allows a person or an organisation to keep thriving rather than merely surviving through whichever one arrives next.
Simon Thomas, Founder
Lineage

Two eras, one standard.

Two trophies, two decades apart. Ashley Jones built and led the Crusaders performance programme through the club's early 2000s era of sustained success. Simon led it through the late 2010s and early 2020s. The photograph matters because it captures something a results table cannot.

Championship environments are inherited. What Ashley built did not end when he left, it became the standard the next group was measured against and the foundation the next era was built on. That observation sits underneath everything ST Performance does. A department is not a person, it is a set of conditions, and the job of whoever holds it is to hand it over in better shape than they found it.

It is the reason ST Performance is built around growing people rather than building dependence on any one practitioner. The measure of a leader is not what the group achieves while they are there. It is what it keeps achieving after they leave.

How Simon Thinks

The ideas behind the work

ST Performance is not built on borrowed slogans. The work draws on a small set of research anchors that have survived twenty years of contact with real environments: Taleb's antifragility, the Stulberg and Magness growth equation of stress plus rest, Edmondson's psychological safety research and the Job Demands-Resources model.

Around those anchors sit original frameworks developed and published through ST Performance, including Shadow Load, the Risk Avoidance Cascade, the Values-Culture Gap and the Balcony Discipline. Each one exists because a real team needed it before it had a name.

None of it is theory borrowed from a book and applied unchanged. Each anchor earned its place by surviving contact with a real department under real pressure, which is also why the frameworks below it are practical rather than academic.

By Design, Simon's forthcoming book on building performance environments deliberately, brings the full methodology together across sixteen chapters. It draws on eight documented seasons of his theming and culture work from Canterbury, the Crusaders and Major League Rugby.

Non-Negotiable

Three values, locked

Most organisations write values once and then never test a decision against them. These three are load bearing. They decide who gets taken on, how engagements are scoped and what gets refused.

01

People First

The environment exists for the people inside it, not the other way round. Every system we build has to answer to that or it does not get built.

02

Meaning Over Status

Titles do not create impact and seniority does not confer judgement. The work is measured by what changes for people, not by who was in the room.

03

Rigour is Respect

Clear expectations, honest feedback and deliberate development are how you show people you take them seriously, because vagueness is not a form of kindness.
